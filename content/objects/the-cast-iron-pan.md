Most objects are at their best the day you bring them home. A cast-iron pan is at its worst.

It improves with every meal. Oil polymerises into a surface no factory coating can match, and after a few years it becomes something closer to an heirloom than a tool.

## Care, not chores

The rules are simple, and they're the same ones that apply to most good things:

1. Use it often.
2. Clean it gently.
3. Dry it completely.
4. Give it a little oil before you put it away.

:::aside
It's hard to ruin a cast-iron pan. Even rust is just an invitation to start again.
:::

There's something reassuring about an object that rewards consistency over perfection.
