It is an unremarkable object at first glance: a brass barrel, a simple clip, no logo worth mentioning. It has been in my pocket for twelve years.

![A brass pen on linen, its finish worn to a soft shine](/images/pen.svg "Twelve years of pockets have given the brass a finish no factory could.")

## Why it stayed

| Quality | What it means in practice |
| :-- | :-- |
| Weight | It sits in the hand without being noticed |
| Refills | A standard cartridge, available anywhere |
| Material | Brass ages instead of wearing out |
| Repair | Nothing glued — every part unscrews |

Most things we buy are designed to be replaced. This one was designed to be *kept*, and you can feel the difference every time you pick it up.

:::note
The patina is the point. Each scratch is a record of somewhere it's been.
:::

## Buying once

Buying once usually means paying more upfront, waiting longer, and choosing more carefully. It also means fewer decisions over a lifetime — which, it turns out, is the real luxury.
