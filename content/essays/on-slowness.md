There is a particular pleasure in a task done at its natural pace: bread that rises for as long as it needs, a letter written by hand, a walk with no destination.

## The cost of hurry

Hurry doesn't only make us faster. It makes us *thinner* — spread across more things, present in fewer of them. We arrive everywhere slightly out of breath.

:::callout An experiment
For one week, pick a single daily task and do it at half speed. Coffee, a commute, the dishes. Notice what you notice.
:::

## Slowness as a skill

Going slowly is not the same as being idle. A craftsperson works slowly because each step depends on the last; rushing one ruins the next. Slowness, in that sense, is just attention extended through time.

The world will keep accelerating. We don't have to match it everywhere. We only have to choose the few places where we won't.
