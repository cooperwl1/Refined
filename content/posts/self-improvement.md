Around 4 years ago I started my YouTube channel Cooper Codes. Starting a YouTube channel is lonely, confusing, and massively difficult. Throughout this process I found myself constantly turning towards online content creators as part of my daily life, even getting to the point where I would watch self improvement content during my lunches and dinners. 

In the moment I thought I was doing the right thing, I was learning, I was "figuring it out", when in the end I was more lost and broke than I had ever been in my life.

This is a personal failing in some parts, but I do think that the allure of self improvement culture results in a majority swarm of people who stay stuck in careers they hate, businesses they hate, a financial situation they hate, all because they need to "lock in" and "embrace the grind". When I was a YouTuber, I felt that I couldn't have a normal job and that I needed to become this entrepreneurial idea of myself that honestly did not exist yet.

:::callout Who did you watch?
Hamza, a handful of NoFap creators who I don't even remember the names of, Chris Williamson, Dr. K, Joe Rogan, Jordan Peterson, and similar names. Although this is less about certain individuals, and more about online self improvement culture as a whole.
:::

## Some Things You Can't "Improve" Out Of

Let's use the example of a business. Let's say that you hate this business, you hate working on it, you hate the people, and it stresses you out. If you "improve" the business by working on it, you effectively are pouring time into something you hate. You create more of what you hate.

Sometimes in our lives we don't require improvement, we require change. Not improvement, not self destruction (contrary to fight club), but instead change.

The unfortuante truth is that for many people the change isn't what they want to hear, they need to start working at McDonalds, living in a shitty apartment, and get rid of their leased Honda Civic. Stop eating out, stop hanging with the hot girlfriend, yada yada.

## Money Matters (What Self Improvers Ignore)

Self improvement conveniently ignores the topic of money, except when they tell you how "easy" it is to make money once you just get the right "mindset". I'm here to tell you that this is bullshit. We all come from different monetary backgrounds, some of us have broke parents, and homelessness or worse could genuinely happen if we take too significant of a risk. 

Money matters. Money super fucking matters. For 99% of people, taking risks in business generally stems from being able to have enough cushion to make sure that you survive the fall. Burning the boats is a great mindset to have in general, and working hard should be your priority, but giving yourself ZERO cushion just results in a crash landing. Take risks, and if you need to then zero out, but don't zero out in a way that causes massive debts, massive collapse, or massive poverty.

Part of the idea of Refined is discussing the idea of abundance alonside creative and financial success. I disagree with the idea of the broke entrepreneur. Money is an incredibly valuable thing, and even a small safety net of 6 months of expenses can allow you to pursue business in a more meaningful, unique, and financially rewarding way. Playing it safe isn't fun I get it, but I think more people getting into the game of business need to hear that YOU NEED MONEY. Venture capitalists, your parents, your friends, your brokerage account, your Pokemon collection.... you need to get money from somewhere.

Going to zero in your bank account sucks, let's call it zeroing out. Zeroing out is not fun, zeroing out does not result in good business, zeroing out means that you will destroy industry connections. I've been there, I've been the guy with the connects and I betrayed their trust by acsking for side gigs and other work during my rock bottom. My neediness only resulted in them thinking "what the hell happened to this guy?". Entrepreneurs are a ruthless bunch, once they see your weakness they will quickly capitlize on it. If you're getting in this game and this happens to you, don't say I didn't warn you.
